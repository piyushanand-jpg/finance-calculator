What is a SIP?

A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) lets you invest a fixed amount into a mutual fund every month, instead of putting in a lump sum. Over time, this builds discipline, smooths out market ups and downs (called rupee-cost averaging), and lets compounding work in your favor — the longer you stay invested, the faster your wealth tends to grow in the later years.

How is SIP return calculated?

The calculator above uses the standard compound growth formula for a series of monthly investments:

M = P × [(1+i)^n − 1] / i × (1+i)

Where M is the maturity amount, P is your monthly investment, i is the monthly rate of return, and n is the total number of months invested.

Why starting early matters

  • A SIP started 10 years earlier can outgrow one with double the monthly amount, purely due to compounding time.
  • Small, consistent monthly investments often beat trying to time the market with lump sums.
  • Increasing your SIP amount gradually as your income grows (a "step-up SIP") can significantly boost your final corpus.
